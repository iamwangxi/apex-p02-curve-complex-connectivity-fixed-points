# Simultaneous isotopy of marked arc systems

This file proves the representative-independence input used in [the natural arc retraction](04-equivariant-arc-retraction.md). Arcs are unoriented subsets; a loop has two tracked half-edges at its marked basepoint. Departure directions are allowed to rotate. The statement is restricted to the target paper's smooth regular representatives (Convention 2.7, printed p. 5).

The literature inputs are single-arc hyperbolic representatives and homotopy-to-isotopy, as stated in Farb–Margalit §1.2.7, printed pp. 35–36, and Epstein Theorems 3.1 and 4.1, pp. 89–91, with Appendix A1–A2, pp. 105–106. Epstein 3.1 is used only after truncation creates genuine boundary endpoints. The proof below supplies the bridge from component classes to a common system isotopy, including cyclic order and integral cusp winding.

**Lemma 2.1 (simultaneous isotopy of essential arc systems on the marked sphere).** Let $`B`$ be a set of six points in an oriented smooth sphere. Let $`(\alpha_i)_{i=1}^n`$ and $`(\beta_i)_{i=1}^n`$ be finite systems of smooth essential arcs, regular at their endpoints in $`B`$, allowing both ends of an arc to coincide. Within each system the interiors are pairwise disjoint and the arcs represent distinct ambient-isotopy classes relative to $`B`$. If $`\alpha_i`$ and $`\beta_i`$ are ambient isotopic relative to $`B`$ for every $`i`$, there is a topological ambient isotopy $`H_t`$ of the sphere such that $`H_0=\mathrm{id}`$, $`H_t|_B=\mathrm{id}`$, and $`H_1(\alpha_i)=\beta_i`$ for all $`i`$. No tangent direction at a marked point is prescribed.

**Proof.** Equip $`S^2\setminus B`$ with a complete finite-area hyperbolic metric. We use only the single-arc fact that an essential proper arc has a unique geodesic representative in its marked isotopy class, including essential based loops; see Farb–Margalit §1.2.7, pp.35–36, with the boundary-arc and based-loop homotopy-to-isotopy statements in Epstein 3.1 and 4.1. Thus each lift of an essential arc has two distinct parabolic ideal endpoints, and its ideal endpoint pair determines its individual arc class. The stabilizer in the torsion-free deck group of such an unordered pair is trivial: a parabolic stabilizer fixes no second point, and an element interchanging the two points would have trivial square.

Use the filled cusp coordinates $`q=\exp(2\pi iz/L)`$ of this metric, whose circles are precisely the projected horocycles; transport the compactified smooth structure to the prescribed marked smooth sphere if necessary. Normalize the finitely many tails of each system radially in these coordinates by local ambient isotopies fixing the marked centers, and choose disjoint cusp neighborhoods meeting the systems only in these tails. In the universal cover fix a cusp lift $`x=\infty`$, with horoball $`\{\mathrm{Im}z\ge R\}`$ and stabilizer $`z\mapsto z+L`$. Each incident lifted half-edge is uniquely labeled by its component $`i`$ and its other ideal endpoint $`y\in\mathbb R`$. The two systems have the same such labels because the corresponding individual arcs are isotopic. Distinctness of the component classes and triviality of the pair stabilizer exclude duplicate labels, including the two half-edges of a loop.

For either system, the order of its intersections with the horocycle agrees with the order of the corresponding $`y`$'s. Indeed, truncate two lifted arcs at that horocycle and add their finite ideal endpoints. The resulting compact arcs avoid $`\infty`$, so both lie in a sufficiently wide rectangle $`[-K,K]\times[0,R]`$, meeting its top and bottom sides only at their respective endpoints and missing its vertical sides. Reversed endpoint order would force an intersection by Jordan separation. The label-preserving match of the two intersection sets is therefore increasing and commutes with $`u\mapsto u+L`$. Extend it by interpolation to an increasing homeomorphism $`f_x`$ of $`\mathbb R`$ with $`f_x(u+L)=f_x(u)+L`$. This match of the entire lifted sequence, not just its quotient circle, fixes the possible integral winding.

Extend $`f_x`$ to a collar isotopy by the formula
```math
(u,r)\longmapsto\bigl((1-t\lambda(r))u+t\lambda(r)f_x(u),r\bigr),
```
where $`\lambda=0`$ at the outer collar and $`\lambda=1`$ at and inside the truncation circle. Use deck translates on the other cusp lifts. The periodicity makes this equivariant. It descends, extends over each marked center by the identity on that center, and begins at the identity. After this isotopy the corresponding radial tails agree. More precisely, lifts paired by their ideal endpoints meet both of their truncation horocycles at exactly the same points.

Let $`M`$ be the sphere with the six cusp neighborhoods removed. The horoballs may be chosen a fixed positive distance $`\delta`$ inside pairwise disjoint larger open horoballs. The smaller closed horoballs are then at least $`2\delta`$ apart and locally finite. Retraction along each cusp coordinate, fixed on the horoball boundaries and outside the horoballs, is therefore a jointly continuous equivariant strong deformation retraction. Their exterior in $`\mathbb H^2`$ is consequently the simply connected universal cover of $`M`$. The corresponding truncated arcs, whose paired lifts have the same two finite endpoints, are homotopic with endpoints fixed. Each loop has now become an ordinary proper interval with two distinct boundary endpoints. Within either system all these boundary endpoints are distinct.

Epstein 3.1 converts each of these relative homotopies to a boundary-fixed isotopy. Apply Farb–Margalit Lemma 2.9, pp.61–62: the truncated arcs are essential, pairwise nonisotopic and pairwise disjoint even at their endpoints, so its minimal-position and triple conditions hold literally. It yields one simultaneous ambient isotopy of $`M`$ relative to its boundary. Equivalently, its bigon induction can fix all previously aligned components pointwise: a bigon or the final disk bounded by the next pair cannot meet a previous proper arc, since its boundary misses that arc and its interior contains no boundary endpoint.

Extend this last isotopy by the identity across the deleted marked disks and concatenate with the collar isotopy and the initial tail normalizations. This produces the required ambient isotopy of the marked sphere. $`\square`$


## Why the hypotheses are needed

Three parallel isotopic arcs with a prescribed reversed component matching can violate the cyclic order at their shared endpoint. Distinct component classes exclude this ambiguity. Matching only positions on a truncation circle also misses an integral boundary twist; the match of the entire periodic lifted sequence records it. A based loop contributes two distinct lifted half-edges, not one. The final isotopy starts at the identity and fixes every marked point; a homeomorphism carrying the graph correctly need not have this property.

The construction allows disconnected systems, isolated marks and nested loops. It supplies no simultaneous-isotopy statement for systems with interior intersections. The common-isotopy input for a pair of intersecting closed curves used later is instead Farb–Margalit Lemma 2.9, whose triple condition is vacuous for two members.
