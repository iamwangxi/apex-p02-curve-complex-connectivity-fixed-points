# Bibliography and inspected versions

All page references below are printed page numbers. Public URLs identify the publications; the preparation used retained local copies and made no new network requests. No third-party full text or PDF is bundled. The target is identified by its exact hash. Classical results are used in their stated scope, not claimed as independently reproved theories.

Acquisition: retained public book PDF. Relevant passages: primitive torus slopes and intersection formula, pp. 27, 29; Corollary 1.9 and the following simultaneous-minimal-position paragraph, p. 33; Proposition 1.10, p. 34; arc versions and endpoint conventions, §1.2.7, pp. 35–36; surface classification/change of coordinates, §1.3, pp. 36–38; Proposition 2.8, p. 59, and Lemma 2.9, pp. 61–62; Proposition 3.2, p. 69. ## 6. Dan Margalit and Rebecca R. Winarski

[*The Birman–Hilden Theory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03448v1), arXiv:1703.03448v1, 9 March 2017. Acquisition: retained public arXiv PDF. Inspected pp. 1–2 and 6: the genus-two exact sequence and $`\mathrm{SMod}(S_2)=\mathrm{Mod}(S_2)`$, with sphere mapping classes allowing marked-point permutations. Fiber-preserving representatives make the arc-lift dictionary equivariant. Class-level triviality of the deck involution is deduced here using centrality and invariant models for both curve types. No inspection of the original Haas–Susskind article is claimed.
